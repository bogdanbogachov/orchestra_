Literature review and proposed research gap — 28 October 2026

This focused review supports the continuation of the intent-classification work described in `rework.md`, the repository README, and *The Hidden States Cookbook*. It combines three review roles (paper discovery, methods critique, and venue/application fit), primary-source searches, and examination of methods in the closest papers. It is a targeted narrative review, not an exhaustive systematic review. A candidate gap means that the reviewed papers do not establish the proposed result; it does not prove that no related paper exists. Preprints are identified where a published venue was not verified.

**Recommendation.** Investigate whether partial layer trajectories can predict the harm of removing a set of tokens under conversational nuisance changes, and use that prediction to control actual intermediate-layer pruning. Evaluate both resistance to harmless additions and sensitivity to verified intent-changing edits, together with measured inference cost. The central contribution must be an experimentally established relationship between representation dynamics, contextual mixing, and pruning reliability. A new loss combination or a switch to Llama is insufficient by itself.

**Closest literature and its implications**

| Work | Established contribution relevant here | Implication for the follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Wang & Kuo, **SBERT-WK** (2020) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06652) | Combines representations across BERT layers; uses variation in cross-layer cosine similarity to weight words. Reports that high-variation words tend to be more informative, with an IDF correlation. | This is the relevant 2020 precedent behind the proposal. It supports motivation, but does not validate early-prefix trajectory scores as task-specific pruning decisions in causal classifiers. |
| Goyal et al., **PoWER-BERT** (ICML 2020) — [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/goyal20a.html) | Progressively removes intermediate word vectors, learns retention, and evaluates BERT classification efficiency. | Intermediate text-token elimination for classification is established. |
| De Cao et al., **DiffMask** (EMNLP 2020) — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.262/) | Learns token inclusion decisions from intermediate hidden representations while preserving model predictions; studies BERT classification and QA. | Hidden-state gates and minimal sufficient selections are established. Attribution masking is not automatically an execution speedup. |
| Chang et al., **Invariant Rationalization** (ICML 2020) — [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/chang20c.html) | Selects rationales intended to support invariant predictions across environments and reduce reliance on spurious correlations. | Invariant token selection is not a new principle. |
| Ye et al., **TR-BERT** (NAACL 2021) — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.463/) | Learns dynamic token reduction and different computation depths for tokens. | Allocating less depth to some tokens is established. |
| Modarressi et al., **AdapLeR** (ACL 2022) — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.1/) | Learns token contribution predictors for adaptive length reduction in BERT. | Learned task-conditioned token saliency has strong baselines. |
| Chen et al., **Can Rationalization Improve Robustness?** (NAACL 2022) — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.278/) | Tests rationalizers under additive irrelevant text across five tasks; finds both robustness benefits and failures related to position and lexical choices. | “Remove irrelevant text to improve robustness” is already studied. Position and vocabulary shortcuts must be controlled. |
| Treviso et al., **CREST** (ACL 2023) — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.842/) | Joint framework for selective rationalization and counterfactual text generation, including counterfactual training. | Counterfactual supervision for selection is also established. |
| Qiang et al., **PPCL** (Findings EACL 2024) — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-eacl.91/) | Trains LLM intent classification and slot filling with consistency across perturbed inputs; evaluates oronyms, synonyms, and paraphrases. | Clean/noisy consistency in LLM intent classification is a required baseline, not the new contribution. |
| Qi et al., **Practical Token Pruning for Foundation Models in Few-shot Conversational Virtual Assistant Systems** (2024 preprint) — [paper and methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11799v1) | Applies attention-based pruning to enterprise intent classification; chooses pruning depth, quantile, and minimum input length through adaptation. | Intent classification, production deployment, and pruning efficiency already occur together. It explicitly treats short utterances as a case where pruning may not pay off. |
| Pan et al., **LLMLingua-2** (Findings ACL 2024) — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.57/) | Learns extractive prompt compression via token classification and evaluates generalization and total latency. | Input compression is a competing solution; its preprocessing cost must be included in comparisons. |
| Ben Artzy & Schwartz, **Attend First, Consolidate Later** (BlackboxNLP 2024) — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.blackboxnlp-1.10/) | Manipulates previous-token representations and finds their influence depends strongly on decoder depth. | A token's own representation change and its continuing usefulness to other tokens are different quantities. |
| Italiani et al., **Read Between the Tokens / PRUNEPERT** (IEEE Transactions on Audio, Speech and Language Processing, 2025; extends a Findings ACL 2024 paper) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASLPRO.2025.3629289), [full text](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/9815b0a8-92fa-4a66-a783-2b5427f22a6f/Read_Between_the_Tokens_Differentiable_Text_Pruning_via_Perturbed_Top-k_Selection.pdf) | Places a learned scorer and differentiable top-k selector between layers. Classification experiments include Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B, and Gemma-7B. | This is the closest architecture/task baseline. Decoder-only classification with learned intermediate pruning is already covered. Its scorer uses current hidden representations. |
| Choi et al., **Representation Shift** (ICCV 2025) — [paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Choi_Representation_Shift_Unifying_Token_Compression_with_FlashAttention_ICCV_2025_paper.html) | Uses changes in token representations as importance scores for compression compatible with FlashAttention; evaluates vision/video tasks. | Representation-change scoring itself is not new. Reproduce its score faithfully as a baseline before introducing different normalized features. |
| Li et al., **TransPrune** (2025 arXiv version) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20630) | Uses token-transition magnitude and direction, with instruction-guided attention, to prune visual tokens in large vision-language models. | A trajectory score in a decoder context is also adjacent prior art; its modality and evaluated task differ. The linked arXiv version is the verified reference here. |
| Jin et al., **END: Early Noise Dropping** (2025 preprint) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18915), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18915) | Uses early hidden-state probes to identify and discard noisy chunks, improving context use and computational efficiency in generation tasks. | Early denoising and efficiency are already combined in LLMs. The proposed study must distinguish token-level intent evidence, pruning depth, and realistic nuisance changes. |
| Irpan et al., **Consistency Training Helps Stop Sycophancy and Jailbreaks** (2025 preprint) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27062) | Studies consistency across irrelevant prompt cues using model outputs and internal activations. | Activation consistency alone would not provide a new method contribution. |
| **Adaptive Layer Selection for Layer-Wise Token Pruning in LLM Inference** (Findings ACL 2026) — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.48/) | Adapts layer selection for token pruning using stability of token-importance information across layers. | Selecting pruning depth from layer dynamics cannot be claimed broadly as new. |
| Arthanari et al., **Sparse Token Routing in Efficient Transformers** (August 2026 preprint) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20632) | Evaluates learned routing, actual sparse execution, and counterfactual masking faithfulness; shows the importance of contextual gating and architecture dependence. | Compute plus faithfulness is already an explicit research objective. The new work needs a specific nuisance/semantic-change result and strong contextual-gate controls. |
| He et al., **From Token Importance to Conditional Removability / CoRePrune** (September 2026 preprint) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26484) | Shows that visual-token removability depends on depth and the surrounding deletion set, and proposes pruning based on these effects. | Neither “importance differs from removability” nor group- and depth-dependent deletion is a new general principle. Its application and evidence under conversational text shift remain distinct. |

Publication status matters: a preprint can undermine a novelty claim even when its results have not been independently validated. The table does not treat its reported performance as an independently reproduced fact. No paper's speedup should be transferred numerically to this repository.

**What the review supports as a gap**

The closest work establishes all the individual ingredients: layer-based word scoring, learned Llama pruning, intent-specific pruning, nuisance robustness, and counterfactual rationale training. Therefore an architecture assembled from those ingredients needs a substantive new result.

The candidate question is:

> Under conversational distribution shift, when do the early layer trajectories of a causal classifier predict that a token set can be removed without losing intent evidence, and can a controller exploit that prediction to reduce actual inference cost while preserving both nuisance invariance and sensitivity to intent-changing edits?

The papers reviewed do not establish this joint result. Confidence is high that the component novelty claims are unavailable; confidence is moderate that this particular empirical and methodological question remains insufficiently answered. Its value must be demonstrated against the closest baselines, especially PRUNEPERT with the same robustness training.

Three aspects make the question substantive:

1. **Task information can be carried by small changes.** A word's linguistic informativeness or large representation movement does not establish the cost of removing it for a particular intent decision. Negation, corrections, and contrasting entities are useful stress tests when they genuinely change the dataset label.
2. **Dropping a position cannot automatically erase its earlier influence.** Retained states may already contain information mixed from filler. Conversely, a semantically important source token may become removable after its useful information has propagated elsewhere. This motivates measuring pruning effects at different depths, rather than treating an insertion mask as the true label for token importance.
3. **Causal attention makes placement consequential.** Prefix filler can influence later content; suffix filler can collect useful content information and become important to a last-token readout. Compare prefix, suffix, and valid internal insertions separately. A separator that merely recognizes position is insufficient.

These are hypotheses and architecture-based motivations to investigate, not claims that the proposed experiment has already confirmed them. The broad contextual-mixing observation has prior art; the intended discovery concerns its effect on reliable text pruning under nuisance and semantic changes.

**A method that preserves the existing foundation**

Keep the current Llama classifier and LoRA setup. At a candidate layer, feed a small gate both current hidden states and a short history of normalized changes. Compare cosine drift, relative L2 updates, and SBERT-WK-style variation. Keep a spectrum-based feature only if it adds beyond these simpler features. Use only layers already computed when making the decision.

The proposed extension is to train the gate/controller against the measured harm of actual candidate pruning decisions on training examples. For each candidate retained set, run the remaining layers and measure supervised classification loss on clean, nuisance-perturbed, and verified intent-changing examples. Train a cheap predictor of this harm from the partial trajectories and candidate-set summaries, or use the measurements to supervise the selection policy. Account for interactions among removed tokens; do not assume the sum of isolated token-deletion effects is accurate.

This is a proposed research design. It is not established as superior and should be compared with direct end-to-end gate training. The goal is to make the partial trajectory predict useful decisions under shift, rather than merely assume that small movement means removable.

Use a computation penalty and supervise all examples with their correct labels. Consistency can be applied to validated pairs with the same label; label-changing examples must receive their changed target. Never enforce unchanged predictions on a true intent change. Dense-model imitation alone is insufficient because the dense model can itself be wrong under noise.

In keeping with `rework.md`, use the generator's inserted-span annotations for evaluation and oracle diagnostics, not gate supervision. Training pairs and candidate token masks do not require ground-truth nuisance spans. A later experiment using span supervision would be a separately labelled, additional-supervision condition with equally supervised baselines.

At inference, use one input and one partial forward pass. Physically gather retained tokens for the remaining layers, preserve positional information and the declared readout, and include gate/compaction overhead in measurement. Do not require clean input, paired views, or multiple full-model runs at deployment. Permit no pruning when predicted harm or overhead is too high; a fixed minimum-length bypass is already a known baseline.

**The pilot should test feasibility before training a new gate**

Use an existing trained classifier, not an unfitted classification head on a frozen base model. Freeze this checkpoint for the diagnostic. Reserve source examples and perturbation families independently across development and final evaluation; all variants of one source query stay in one split. Retain the known label inventory for ordinary intent classification—holding out entire intent labels creates a different generalization task.

| Diagnostic | What to compare | Decision it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline headroom | Clean vs naturally padded/controlled noisy accuracy for last-token and the strongest existing attention-pooling baseline | Whether the actual classifier has a robustness problem worth solving. Avoid manufacturing severity just to force an effect. |
| Oracle depth sweep | Remove exactly the injected spans before the model and at several intermediate layers; retain the required readout and state that exception | Whether late removal can recover accuracy and whether useful pruning exists at affordable depths. The span oracle is a diagnostic, not an achievable method or a guaranteed upper bound. |
| Position controls | Prefix/suffix/internal variants; original-position and compact-position controls where relevant | Whether apparent salience or recovery is driven by position, causal context, or RoPE changes. |
| Signal value | Current hidden state, SBERT-WK, Representation Shift, transition features, and their combination; use probes fitted on development data | Whether trajectories add information about observed pruning harm over strong content features. A fitted probe is a small training experiment, even if the backbone is frozen. |
| Meaning sensitivity | Human-checked minimal pairs that change the actual intent label; nuisance variants of both members | Whether selection preserves distinguishing evidence instead of simply becoming insensitive to text changes. |
| Execution feasibility | Actual compacted inference, including gate/gather cost, across natural length buckets | Whether there is a realistic latency/energy benefit for the target workload. |

Insertion-mask AUROC is a secondary diagnostic. Original content contains redundant tokens, and an inserted suffix can become a carrier of useful context. The primary diagnostics are prediction accuracy and the loss incurred by the actual pruning intervention. Evaluate both individual and group deletions.

Stop the trajectory-specific claim if it offers no useful improvement over a current-state gate and simple scores at matched budgets. If exact-span removal at the planned depth does not help, inspect earlier pruning and retained-state mixing before investing in a learned denoiser at that depth. If actual execution is slower on the real length distribution, the efficiency claim is unsupported. A negative result weakens this proposed mechanism, not all token-gating research.

**Decisive baselines and evaluation**

The most informative controlled comparison is a small factorial experiment: current-state gate versus current-state-plus-trajectory gate, each with and without identical paired robustness training. Include PRUNEPERT or a faithful adaptation, with the same backbone, readout, data, LoRA budget, and token budget. This exposes whether the benefit comes from trajectories, extra augmentation, or simply training a selector.

Additional controls: the best dense classifier with augmentation alone; dense PPCL-style consistency; random pruning at the same depth/retention; attention scoring; adapted SBERT-WK/Representation Shift/TransPrune scores; Qi et al.'s intent-pruning recipe; simple input cleanup; LLMLingua-2 including compressor cost; and a strong small encoder classifier. An expensive gradient/deletion oracle is useful for analysis but is not an equal-cost deployment baseline. Include an END-style chunk selector if longer multi-sentence inputs are part of the task.

Begin with BANKING77 and CLINC150, then add a third domain and real conversational messages or real ASR transcripts with verified intents. Standard curated intent text is not automatically naturally noisy. Candidate resources include [CLINC150](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1131/), [BANKING77](https://aclanthology.org/2020.nlp4convai-1.5/), and [DialoGLUE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13570). Production-style noise has its own prior evaluation, e.g. [Sengupta et al. (2021)](https://aclanthology.org/2021.nlp4convai-1.7/). Do not conflate irrelevant additions with ASR errors that destroy important evidence: dropping tokens cannot restore missing information.

Report clean and noisy macro-F1, worst noise-family accuracy, and the fraction of nuisance pairs for which both predictions are correct. Prediction agreement alone can reward a consistently wrong classifier. For label-changing pairs, report the fraction for which both predictions match their respective gold labels. Token retention of annotated spans is explanatory evidence, not the principal success measure.

Measure latency, throughput, and energy per query on the same hardware/precision at batch 1 and realistic batch sizes. Report length buckets and total workload averages, scoring overhead, packing overhead, and measured training cost. Match accuracy and runtime budgets as well as token counts; a matched token count need not imply a matched cost. Use the existing matched-seed protocol and paired uncertainty estimates, clustering augmented variants by source example. Choose tolerances for clean-accuracy loss and minimum practical savings before inspecting final results.

**Position relative to the current work and venues**

[The Hidden States Cookbook](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05394) already evaluates pooling and FFT variants under conversational noise. The follow-up's contribution would be a tested account of when intermediate token deletion is reliable, a gate/controller informed by that account, and demonstrated reductions in inference execution. Repeating the pooling comparison with another saliency weight would create a much smaller delta. Neither the current negative FFT result nor this review establishes that spectral methods fail in general.

For ACL, a convincing route is a reproducible phenomenon plus a method that improves the accuracy/robustness/runtime frontier over the controlled PRUNEPERT and consistency baselines. ICLR would require a broader adaptive-computation insight across tasks or model families; that is a higher-risk target for an intent-only study. Theory is optional, but evidence of generality and a clear contribution are necessary.

For Advanced Engineering Informatics, evaluate a genuine knowledge-intensive engineering workflow, such as maintenance or engineering-support ticket triage, with its actual label semantics and operational costs. Generic banking classification plus an energy metric does not supply that application. The journal's [AI for the Built World editorial](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1474034625011188) illustrates engineering-focused NLP, decision support, and infrastructure-maintenance work; venue-fit advice here is an inference from that remit, not an acceptance prediction.

A provisional research title is **Layerwise Token Dynamics for Robust and Efficient Intent Classification**. A defensible proposal sentence is: “We investigate whether partial representation trajectories can predict the effects of intermediate token pruning under conversational nuisance shifts, and whether this signal supports lower-cost classification while preserving responses to intent-changing evidence.” Results, rather than the combination of established ingredients, must support a stronger claim.
